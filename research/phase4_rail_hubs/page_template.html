What drives the screen. Default recipes for the four Phase 4 parts expand to iron, copper, crude oil, coal, limestone, water, caterium, bauxite, raw quartz and nitrogen gas. Sulfur and uranium do not appear in that chain; uranium matters only if you go nuclear for power. Phase 5 adds SAM.
Roles. Aluminum: bauxite within 400 m of mapped water, plus quartz and coal nearby. Oil: crude nodes or fracking. Nitrogen: gas wells. Electronics: caterium and quartz together. Bulk: iron, copper and coal together. SAM: SAM nodes. Top six per role, spaced at least 800 m apart, then grouped into hubs within 750 m.
Weights are opinion. Purity counts as impure 0.5, normal 1, pure 2, and a fracking satellite counts like a node. The role scores are a ranking I chose for this screen, not a repo policy.
Reading the grades. Two repo sources disagree on rail grade: the route tool's rail profile blocks at 8%, while route_constraints.csv cites 28 m of rise per 94.5 m (about 30%) for a flat-ended segment. Both are shown. Most links spend half their length above 8%, so ground-following track is not realistic; expect foundations or pillars, with the corridor as the alignment to follow.
Rail mode. The route tool's rail profile (8% hard grade) finds no ground route on any link, and 15% clears only S2–C2. At 30% (the route_constraints.csv figure, declared for this run and not committed) seven links return a route, but only S2–C2 passes the tool's own 5 m validation; the rest keep 14–104 m of steeper-than-30% spots, and C1–S2 and S1–W1 have no route even with bridges allowed. Searches were cropped to 1,200 m around each link.
Player guidance (r/SatisfactoryGame, about 2 years old). Two-metre foundation ramps are the safe slope (2 m per 8 m foundation, 25%); 4 m ramps make trains struggle; keep to 4 cars per engine on those slopes. At 25%, every link's net rise fits in 4–36% of its corridor length, so the corridors carry ramped track. Foundations are needed mainly where the ground itself is steeper than 25%.
Ramp classes. The map is cut into 8 m cells (one foundation), each the mean of the 1 m heightfield. Track may step to any of 8 neighbours if the rise is within 1 m, 2 m or 4 m per 8 m (12.5%, 25%, 50%). Water deeper than 3 m or of unknown depth blocks. No cuts or tunnels. The shaded reach shows which ground connects to C1 at each class.
Spans. Where a class leaves a hub cut off, a span is a straight deck at that class's grade between the two areas, kept only if the ground never rises more than 2 m above the deck. Ranked by length plus twice the tallest pillar; spans cost 1.5× their length in routing.
Limits. Paths can turn at every cell, so they ignore rail turn radius; real track will be longer, and tight zigzags may not be buildable. Spans are only searched to join cut-off areas, not as shortcuts.
Not assessed. Rail curvature, bridging cost, buildable platform at the hub, socket exclusivity, and whether a link crosses water. Elevations are of the seed sockets, not the ground between them. Water comes from the basemap's transparent pixels, which include two known cave-mouth defects.
